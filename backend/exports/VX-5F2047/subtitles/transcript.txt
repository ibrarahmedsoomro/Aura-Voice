zero:00–zero:twenty — Hook
nineteen thirty-nine. Europe stood on the edge of catastrophe. Within months, a conflict that began in Europe would expand across continents and oceans, drawing dozens of nations and hundreds of millions of people into the deadliest war in human history.
zero:twenty–zero:fifty — The Beginning
On September 1st, nineteen thirty-nine, Nazi Germany invaded Poland. Britain and France responded by declaring war on Germany. What followed was a rapid German offensive across Europe, using coordinated tanks, aircraft, and infantry in a strategy that became known as Blitzkrieg.
zero:fifty–one:twenty — Europe Falls
By nineteen forty, German forces had occupied much of Western Europe. France fell, while Britain resisted through the Battle of Britain. In the skies above Britain, the Royal Air Force fought to prevent a German invasion.
But the war was about to become even larger.
one:twenty–one:fifty — The War Becomes Global
In June nineteen forty-one, Germany launched Operation Barbarossa, invading the Soviet Union and opening the massive Eastern Front.
Then, on December 7th, nineteen forty-one, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor. The United States entered the war, transforming the conflict into a truly global struggle.
From North Africa to the Pacific, from the Atlantic Ocean to the Eastern Front, enormous armies and fleets were now engaged in battle.
one:fifty–two:twenty — Turning Point
The Axis powers initially appeared unstoppable. But during nineteen forty-two and nineteen forty-three, the momentum began to change.
The Battle of Midway weakened Japanese naval power in the Pacific. In North Africa, Allied forces pushed back Axis armies. And at Stalingrad, Soviet forces surrounded and defeated a major German army.
The tide of the war had begun to turn.
two:twenty–two:45 — D-Day and the Collapse of Nazi Germany
On June 6th, nineteen forty-four, Allied forces launched D-Day, landing on the beaches of Normandy.
The invasion opened a new front in Western Europe. Allied armies pushed toward Germany while Soviet forces advanced from the east.
In May nineteen forty-five, Nazi Germany surrendered.
two:45–three:00 — The Pacific and the Legacy
The war continued against Japan until August nineteen forty-five, when atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. Japan surrendered shortly afterward.
World War II had ended.
More than sixty million people had died. Nations had been destroyed, borders redrawn, and a new global order emerged.
The war was over—but its consequences would shape the world for generations.
